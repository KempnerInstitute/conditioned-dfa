Conditioned Direct Feedback Alignment via Activity and Error Geometry

Direct feedback alignment (DFA) trains hidden layers with fixed random projections of the output error. Even when this feedback provides useful credit, the local weight update can be distorted by unequal scales across activity or error directions. We study this geometric failure mode through a symmetric family of normalized DFA (nDFA): activity conditioning acts on the presynaptic activity, error conditioning acts on the local error, and their combination uses independently selected damping. An aligned linear-model analysis shows how activity conditioning changes spectral learning rates. A separate mode-timing calculation explains why this can help when high-variance directions contain nuisance rather than task signal. Controlled synthetic experiments support this regime dependence. Clean MNIST and Fashion-MNIST confirmations establish a distinct error-side benefit and an additional two-sided gain, including a replication with rectified linear units and softmax outputs. Norm-matching and moment controls distinguish these effects from a scalar step-size change. Additional CIFAR-10 studies retain the activity-side benefit with shared batch normalization and wider networks. The gain over DFA persists under matched computational budgets, although backpropagation remains stronger and forward decorrelation is competitive. The error factor depends on damping and training protocol; without appropriate normalization, accuracy gains can coexist with extreme predictive losses. Convolutional credit assignment remains a separate limitation. These results identify activity and error geometry as complementary determinants of learning with fixed feedback, without implying a general replacement for backpropagation.
